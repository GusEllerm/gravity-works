## Item 3 — the crane's legs are painted (visuals only)

The between-room legs of `startFarewell`'s crane flew over the bare background: the sets' own ground
sheets (20 m each) ended in a hard horizon seam and the lift saw flat discs in a warm void
(`playtest-r3/farewell-mid.png`). Dressing, from parts the build view already knows:

- **The table**: one long `PlaneGeometry` with the build view's own ground material
  (`MeshLambertMaterial('#e6d3b3')`, `world.ts`) stretched under the whole row, riding 8 cm BELOW the
  sets' bottoms — the first attempt sat at −0.02 and z-fighted with the set sheets into stripes, which
  is why the offset is stated, not tuned.
- **The haze**: `THREE.FogExp2` in the scene's own cream, density riding the MOVE's lift sine
  (`craneAtmosphere`, apex `CRANE_HAZE.density = 0.11`). Board and fog share ONE pure predicate,
  `craneInMove(t)`: every DWELL and PULL frame gets board hidden + density 0, so the docked and wide
  frames ship the exact pixels the note predates — lawed by the function, not by tuning luck.
- **The three.js lesson (measured, not assumed)**: `scene.fog` mutations-in-place never reach the
  shaders — the renderer refreshes fog uniforms when the fog REFERENCE changes (`materialProperties.fog
  !== fog`), so a per-frame `fog.near = …` ride silently renders the compile-time distances forever.
  Fixed by swapping a fresh `FogExp2` per frame; value-cheap, hits toons and Lambert alike. (A first
  probe wrongly blamed `USE_FOG`-less program caching — the diagnostics fields I read were the wrong
  ones; the pixel-probe verdict (fog forced to near-zero far, screenshot unchanged) is what localized
  it to the uniform refresh. Linear `THREE.Fog` also banded into contour rings over the big sheets at
  8-bit; `FogExp2`'s quadratic falloff doesn't.)
- Evidence: `playtest-r3b/farewell-leg{1..5}.png` — one screenshot per leg at plan t = 1.8/3.7/5.6/
  7.5/9.4 (timed through `__gwFarewellState().t`, wall-clock polling abandoned after it shot docked
  frames). Legs read as flights over a dressed table with the next room resolving out of the haze;
  dwells visibly unchanged. No sim, no plan, no pose touched; `replay:all` 30/30 byte-verified.

## Item 4 — the two chrome debts at 633

**(a) The fail band rode the chips.** Measured FAIL state at 1280×633 on this tree: band `567–623`,
ghost bar + Save chip `608–629`, hash line `580–593`, callout `564–579` — the band crossed ALL of
them, not just the chips (the verdict named the chips; the flow rows were riding too). The fix is the
farewell's own idiom: while the failure band speaks, `stepBandChrome` hides the four lines that share
its rect (`#gw-save`, `#gw-ghost-bar`, `#gw-hash-details`, `#gw-callout` — all four re-say what the
band says or wait for the next run), `hide` restores, the status line stays, and the compact CSS caps
the band (`bottom: 4px; max-height: 65px`) so its top can never climb into the status row on any font.
Re-measured: band `572.6–629`, status ends 563, everything else honestly hidden. `p4-chrome.spec.ts`
grew the FAIL-state pairwise-disjoint assertion (hidden checked as hidden, not z-buried) and
`result.spec.ts`'s round-7 caption test now asserts the callout's step-aside instead of its position.

**(b) The sandbox rules line overprinted its closure.** Root cause was NOT the copy — the sandbox's
thirteen ×99-counted tray labels make an honest TWO-row tray at compact sizes (+25 px of flow the
one-row law can't promise there), which pushed the whole band down: hash line `605–618` inside the
chips `608–629` (measured; the Stars callout itself was disjoint). The 25 px now come from the canvas
on sandbox pages only: boot marks `body[data-gw-sandbox]` (from `CAMPAIGN` sandboxIds, zero hash/save
footprint) and the cap becomes `100vh − 313px` there. Re-measured 633: status `548–563`, callout
`564–579`, hash `580–593`, chips `608–629`, scrollHeight 633 — pairwise disjoint, the disclosure
legible, no copy rewritten. `p4-chrome.spec.ts` asserts the sandbox rects and keeps a wrap-detector
(the reservation must be re-derived if a future change makes the tray one row again).
