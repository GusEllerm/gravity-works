# src/render (material system)

> [!abstract] Role
> The Gravity Works toon material system: one shader-driven material, the seven art-bible classes built on it, the shared procedural mesh generators, and the palette tokens that keep CSS and materials from drifting.

## What it does

`src/render/toon-material.ts` defines `ToonMaterial`, a `THREE.ShaderMaterial` (with `lights: true`) assembled from Three.js shader chunks — `lights_pars_begin` and `lights_fragment_begin` drive the light loop and `RE_Direct_Toon` supplies the toon BRDF. It is deliberately not `MeshToonMaterial`. One deliberate substitution since the bedroom lamp (stage 4): the `lights_fragment_begin` INCLUDE is replaced by a verbatim copy of its point/directional sections that flips a `gPunctualLight` flag around the BRDF call — the punctual gate. The tinted-shadow swap (shadowed fragments keep the set's `shadowTint` instead of going dark) is a DIRECTIONAL concept: a punctual light's color carries distance falloff, so re-adding the key-strength tint wherever a practical fails to reach washes the room back to the stage-3 disease. With the gate a practical adds only its attenuated color; directional-only scenes take the identical math (the kitchen baselines are the byte-identity gate). Parameters follow the art bible: base color, a 2–3 step ramp (`steps` / `thresholds` / `softness`), specular size/strength, rim strength/size, and optional treatments: `toy` (dip-paint gradient), `grain` (procedural painted-wood grain) with a per-surface `grainScale` frequency, `liquid` (time-driven normal wobble), plus the two stage-3 backlog dials `fillStrength` (fill gain, defaulting to the old shader constant 0.25) and `shadowDither` (the shadow-dither budget), and from the stage-4 garden round 1 the parameter `fillShadeDepth` (default 1, `uFillShadeDepth` — the fraction of the fill a SHADOWED directional fragment keeps; see the lighting note below). No image textures anywhere.

`src/render/materials.ts` exports the seven classes as factories taking the set's `SetTokens`: `dieCastPaint`, `trackPlastic`, `paintedWood`, `ceramic`, `fabric`, `glass`, `liquid`. Class-level backlog fixes live here: `ceramic` applies `ceramicSaturationLift` (glaze reads through the gold key), and grain is class-restricted — it survives only on painted wood or toy-treated surfaces, so speckle can never land on glaze again. The stage-3 review round moved two exposure fixes INTO the classes (decision-5 discipline — never per-scene): `ceramic` is now a THREE-band ramp (steps 0.58/0.8/1.0, thresholds 0.25/0.62, softness 0.08) with its broad specular down to strength 0.3, so the full-brightness glaze band lands only on the small wall arc facing the key and the middle band carries the roundness (the two-band class had put the whole key-facing bowl wall in one 243–250 blowout); `liquid` narrowed and lifted its specular (size 0.28, strength 1.15) so a flat liquid surface shows ONE bright highlight riding the wobble with the set tint whole, instead of a wide specular band that desaturated the disc to plastic grey.

`src/render/lighting.ts` is the set lighting rig: `createLightingRig` builds the one shadowed key (bias pair tuned together with the shader's dither budget) and derives the two fill bands pulled toward the set's accent at a parameterized `fillStrength` — the fill can finally *reach* the accent in shade. Two stage-4 outdoor options (garden): an optional `keyColor` lets an outdoor set tint the KEY with the sun's own hue (golden-hour amber) — every indoor set omits it and stays byte-identical — and an OUTDOOR regime derives the sky-side fill band and the shadow tint from the SKY value rather than the set's dominant hue, because outdoors the fill IS the sky (the indoor mix-from-accent rule washed a sunny lawn grey). The garden's round-1 production send-back added the SHADOW half of that idea: `fillShadeDepth` on the rig arrives at the shader as `uFillShadeDepth`, where the directional pass of `RE_Direct_Toon` (the same attenuation the tint swap reads) dims the fill term IN PROPORTION TO SHADOW COVERAGE — an open sky replaces the sun it blocked at full gain unless a set says otherwise, and the sweep behind the send-back proved no global `fillStrength` value can darken shadowed stone without dragging every frame's median (lit pixels are bit-identical at any depth: the factor is an exact 1.0 where nothing is shadowed). Two further opt-in sky-fill dials — `skyFillMix` (how far the sky-side band takes the sky value, default 0.55) and `skyFillShade` (deepen that sky VALUE before mixing, default 0) — default to the first production renders exactly. `applyKeyLight` declares the key's length to every toon material in a scene (the tinted-shadow contract), `fillFromRig` is the scene-side override bundle, and `dustMotes` is an empty hook — §5.5 particles wait for a later juice pass.

`src/render/film.ts` adds the stain/decal capability as FILM, not cutout geometry (the stage-1 send-back): `stainDecal` returns a flat quad (mug ring / wet patch / splash ring shapes are soft SDFs in the shader, with a Fresnel sheen on wet films), transparent, depth-write off, polygon-offset onto the surface — never a squashed sphere lying on the counter.

`src/render/tokens.ts` is the single color source: `SET_TOKENS` (six sets, dominant + accent seeds, with `fillHigh`/`fillLow`/`shadowTint`/`ground`/`background` derived by pure hex math) plus `GLOBAL_TOKENS` (the constant `trackOrange` `#FF7A1A`, cream neutral, key-light color), `cssVars()` for the UI half, and `clampLightness` with `CONTACT_LIGHTNESS_FLOOR` — the warm-brown lightness floor that keeps tyres and contact parts readable through the kitchen grade. The math is dependency-free so Node tests and the browser see identical values.

`src/render/geometry.ts` holds the shared generators the sets will reuse: `toyBlock` (beveled rounded block — the footprint is inset by the bevel because `ExtrudeGeometry` grows the outline by `bevelSize` and the depth by `bevelThickness` at both ends), `bowlForm` (lathe), `trackChannel` (U-channel extrusion; later stages extrude the same cross-section along the track spline).

## How it works

Lighting contract: there is no `AmbientLight` anywhere — fill is the two-band `uFillLow`/`uFillHigh` sampled by world-up (rubric forbids uniform ambient) and scaled by `uFillStrength`, a material parameter since stage 3, not the old shader constant, and (garden round 1) by the shadow-coverage factor `gFillShade` the directional pass writes from `uFillShadeDepth` — an exact 1.0 for every indoor rig and every lit fragment, so only outdoor sets that ask for shade depth ever move it. Scenes therefore add no AmbientLight. Shadow attenuation arrives folded into `directLight.color` by the Three chunk; the shader recovers it against `uKeyLength` (set once per scene via `ToonMaterial.setKeyLight()`) so fully-shadowed faces swap in the set's `shadowTint` at 40% key strength instead of collapsing to ambient black — and partial PCF coverage passes the `uShadowDither` budget first: Three rotates a 5-tap Vogel disc with per-pixel interleaved-gradient noise, whose speckle the budget caps by resolving half-covered fragments toward a slow world-space weave instead of per-pixel noise (the painterly ramp's failure mode, closed on the shadow path too). The snap decision is biased to the shadow side (full light needs ≥ 70 % coverage — snapping a 50/50 penumbra to "lit" smeared bright patches across the tap's floor shadow), and the rig keeps `shadow.normalBias` high enough that curved shells stop half-covering themselves. The specular mask is a half-vector threshold on `specSize` clamped against a hot key (`min(color, 1)`) so highlights cannot blow to white. Backfacing fragments flip the interpolated normal by `gl_FrontFacing` (matching Three's `normal_fragment_begin`), which is what lets a two-sided lathe form — a cereal bowl — shade its inner wall by its true facing instead of sticking in the darkest band.

Scene units are meters at real scale (a 1:64 car is ~0.07 m).

Guarded by `tests/unit/tokens.test.ts` (golden kitchen derivation; hex-math endpoints and symmetry; `cssVars()` held to a one-variable-per-token drift guard against the token object; lightness helpers checked monotonic rather than self-equal — the stage-1 tautologies were removed at stage 2) and, from stage 3, `tests/unit/render-materials.test.ts` (the seven-item material backlog, each item with a named guard) and `tests/unit/render-post.test.ts`. Rendered evidence: docs/explorations/materials/ramp-a.png (hard cel), ramp-b.png (three hard steps), ramp-c.png (painterly) from the stage-1 exploration. Binary renders are intentionally not named in backticks: the drift binder treats a mention as a file anchor and the text-diff checker cannot read binaries.

## Post stack

`src/render/post/` is the composer chain (see `Modules/dev` for the `post=on` harness entry): `tilt-shift.ts` (`TiltShiftPass`: a separable H+V pair at quarter-res in internal buffers plus a full-frame circle-of-confusion composite; `tiltShiftParams` projects a world-space focus point to the band centre and maps its floor distance to defocus strength; band height defaults to 20 % of the frame per §7.3), `bloom.ts` (`SoftBloomPass`, one Pass with quarter-res byte internals, strength hard-clamped to `BLOOM_SOFT_CEILING` — the never-list enforcement is a constant, not a convention), `grade.ts` (`gradeFromTokens` derives the LUT-lite numbers from set tokens by pure hex math; the terminal pass carries the vignette term and the sRGB encode, which retires a separate OutputPass draw), and `index.ts` (`buildPostStages`/`applyQuality`/`createPostStack`; the quality ladder drops bloom → tilt-shift and never resolution). `dispose()` must cover EVERY stage including the terminal `ShaderPass` grade (material + program + fullscreen quad): the game shell's placement rebuild cycles the whole stack (`rebuild()` → `post.dispose()` → `createPostStack`) while `?post=on`, so a missed stage leaks its GPU objects once per piece placed — the stage-3 review found exactly that (grade was omitted) and it is gated by `tests/e2e/post-dispose.spec.ts` (20 build→dispose cycles must return `renderer.info` programs/textures to baseline via the harness `__postCycle` seam; pre-fix the program count ends +1 and pinned, post-fix 8→8). Every design lever here is a frame-cost lever measured under software GL: full-frame draws are the expensive unit, so the stack spends exactly one (the grade) when every dropgable stage is dropped.
